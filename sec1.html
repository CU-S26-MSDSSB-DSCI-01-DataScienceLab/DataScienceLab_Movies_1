Q1

How Do Movie Ratings Vary Across Countries and Industries?

👤 Kay-Lee Dramat
APPROACH

The country column was cleaned and grouped. Ratings were converted to numeric values. Only countries with sufficient data were kept to ensure reliable averages.

Key Finding: Average ratings differ noticeably by country. Smaller or more selective industries rank higher because only stronger, more internationally visible films appear in the dataset. Larger industries like the US show greater variation — from prestige films to mass-market productions.

Q2

Which Genres Receive the Highest Ratings and Most Votes?

👤 Kay-Lee Dramat

Genre values were parsed from list-like text and grouped. Average rating and average vote count were calculated separately, because they measure different things: quality vs. popularity.

Key Finding: Ratings ≠ Votes. Documentary, historical, and epic genres earn the strongest ratings but attract fewer votes. Action and franchise genres drive massive vote counts. A successful genre depends on whether you measure appreciation or audience size.

Q3

How Does Movie Duration Influence Audience Ratings and Votes?

👤 Kay-Lee Dramat

Duration was converted from hours/minutes text into numeric runtime in minutes. The notebook then visualized the relationship between runtime and both rating and votes.

Key Finding: Most movies fall between 80–150 minutes. Within that range, ratings vary widely — runtime alone does not predict whether a movie is highly rated. Audience engagement is more strongly shaped by popularity, genre, and release scale than by length.

Q5

Is There a Relationship Between Budget and Box Office Performance?

👤 Ghofrane Mahmoud

Budget and revenue columns were converted from currency strings to numeric values. ROI = (revenue − budget) / budget. Movies grouped into low (<$10M), mid ($10M–$100M), and high (>$100M) budget tiers.

Key Finding: Pearson r=0.685 (p<0.001) between log-budget and log-worldwide gross. High-budget films have the highest median profitability (65.3% profitable) but require massive returns. Low-budget films can produce spectacular ROI but median ROI is negative.

Q6

Do Movies with Well-Known Personnel Receive Higher Ratings or Votes?

👤 Ghofrane Mahmoud

Stars, directors, and writers were parsed from list-like text. High-profile vs low-profile groups were defined by appearance frequency. Mann-Whitney U tests were used for rating comparisons.

Key Finding: Median rating is 6.30 for both high-profile and low-profile groups across stars, directors, and writers. Even statistically significant results show no practical difference. Star power appears to boost visibility (votes) more than quality evaluation (ratings).
