Q1

Can Movie Rating Be Predicted From Movie Features?

👤 Aya Abdine & Meriam Al Askri
APPROACH

Regression task targeting continuous IMDb rating. Features include genre, duration, budget, votes (log-transformed), release date, MPA rating, stars, and directors. Feature space reduced to top 8 most impactful predictors. Three models compared: Ridge Regression (baseline), Extra Trees, and Gradient Boosting.

Key Finding: Gradient Boosting achieves the best R²=0.365. Log-votes is the dominant predictor (17.7% importance). The model explains ~37% of rating variation — the remainder reflects subjective audience response and story quality that metadata cannot capture.

Q2

Can Box Office Revenue Be Predicted From Movie Characteristics?

👤 Aya Abdine & Meriam Al Askri
APPROACH

Regression task on worldwide gross revenue. Because revenue is extremely skewed, the target was log-transformed to reduce blockbuster influence and stabilize model training. Cleaned numeric and categorical features used. Same three models compared. Log-MAE and raw-scale MAE both reported.

Key Finding: Revenue is far more predictable than rating — Gradient Boosting achieves R²=0.645. The log-transform is critical: training on raw revenue would cause models to overfit to blockbuster extremes. Best raw-scale MAE ≈ $21M.

Q3

👤 Aya Abdine & Meriam Al Askri
APPROACH

Binary classification: high-rated vs not-high-rated. Ambiguous middle ratings removed to create a cleaner binary target. Training data balanced. Decision thresholds tuned away from default 0.50. Four classifiers compared: Logistic Regression (baseline), Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, Extra Trees.

Key Finding: Random Forest achieves ROC AUC=0.873 — the best classifier. The confusion matrix shows strong performance at identifying non-high-rated films. Balanced training and threshold tuning were critical improvements over naive classification.

Q4

Can the Number of Audience Votes Be Predicted?

👤 Mcolisi Austine
APPROACH

Regression task predicting audience vote count using movie popularity factors: stars, release date, runtime, and budget. Vote counts are highly skewed so three strategies were compared: raw prediction, log-transformed prediction, and log prediction after outlier removal. Models: Linear Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest.

Key Finding: Log-transformation dramatically improves the model. After transformation, Decision Tree achieves R²=0.432. Average rating and release year are the most important predictors. Viral attention and marketing hype remain outside the dataset.

Q5

How Does Release Timing Influence Box Office Performance?

👤 Syed Arshan Nehad
APPROACH

Release date parsed into year, month, and season. Season used as the main timing variable. Budget, runtime, crew counts, and primary genre included as controls to isolate timing effects. Worldwide gross and votes both log-transformed. Random Forest and Decision Tree compared.

Key Finding: Random Forest achieves R²=0.606 for audience votes and R²=0.484 for worldwide gross. Budget and release year are more important than season alone — but season still appears in feature importance, especially for audience engagement. Timing matters as part of a larger release strategy, not as a standalone guarantee.

Q6

Can Movies Be Classified as Successful vs Less Successful?

👤 Syed Tamjeed Ur Rahman
APPROACH

Binary success classification combining multiple dimensions: a movie is labeled successful if it meets at least 2 of 3 conditions — rating above median, votes above median, and positive ROI. Leakage variables excluded from predictors. Models: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest.

Key Finding: Random Forest achieves 70.0% accuracy and ROC AUC=0.767 — the best classifier. Budget and year are the top predictors. Accuracy of 70% means the model captures real patterns but cannot fully predict success without marketing, distribution, and streaming data.
