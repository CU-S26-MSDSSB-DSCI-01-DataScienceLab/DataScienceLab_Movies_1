Conclusion

What We Found

After 12 research questions, 6 descriptive analyses, and 6 ML models — here is what the data revealed about movie success.

Core Answer
Movie success is multidimensional

After combining descriptive analysis with machine learning across 12 research questions, the answer is clear: no single feature explains movie success alone. Ratings, votes, and revenue behave differently and are shaped by different combinations of factors.

The Central Research Question

"Which movie characteristics best explain and predict a movie's success in terms of audience evaluation and financial performance?"

Part 1 — Descriptive Analysis
What Patterns Exist in the Data?
🌍 Country Shapes Ratings
Smaller, selective industries show higher averages. Larger ones (US) show greater variation — prestige films alongside mass-market productions.
🎭 Genre: Ratings ≠ Votes
Documentary/biopic genres earn high ratings with fewer votes. Action and franchise genres drive massive audience size regardless of rating quality.
⏱️ Non-linear Runtime Effect
90–140 min films cluster at the highest ratings. Very short or very long films are far more variable. Runtime is not a strong standalone predictor.
💰 Budget → Revenue (Imperfectly)
Pearson r=0.685. High-budget films: 65.3% profitable. Low-budget films: 39.2% profitable. But ROI can go either way — outliers dominate.
⭐ Star Power → Visibility
Well-known personnel boost vote counts and reach. But median ratings are identical (6.30) for high-profile and low-profile groups — fame ≠ quality.
🎬 Action Genres Dominate Revenue
Action Epic ($363M avg), Superhero ($310M), Animation ($284M) lead worldwide gross. International markets are a major driver.

🔑 Key ML Improvement: Log-Transform
Applying log-transform to revenue and vote targets significantly improved all models. Raw skewed targets caused models to over-fit to blockbuster extremes.
🌳 Ensembles Always Win
Random Forest, Extra Trees, and Gradient Boosting consistently outperformed Ridge/Linear baselines by capturing nonlinear interactions between features.
🎯 Feature Selection Matters
Reducing feature space to top 8 predictors improved performance and interpretability. log_votes, budget, and movie_age dominate rating prediction.
⚖️ Balanced Training for Q3
Removing ambiguous middle ratings and balancing the training set produced a much stronger confusion matrix for the high-rating classifier.
Limitations
What the Data Cannot Tell Us
📢Marketing spend and distribution size are not in the dataset — these are major real-world drivers of both revenue and visibility.
🌐Social media attention, critic reviews, franchise history, and streaming availability are missing — all influence modern audience engagement.
📊Appearance frequency is only a proxy for fame. A person appearing in many small films is treated the same as a global celebrity.
🕐The dataset covers a wide time range. Market conditions, audience tastes, and streaming economics have shifted significantly over decades.
🔮Models predict broad revenue levels better than exact values. Viral hits and catastrophic flops remain fundamentally unpredictable from metadata alone.
Final Reflection
Why This Project Matters

This project demonstrates that careful data preparation — parsing text fields, transforming skewed targets, selecting features — matters as much as model selection. Many movie attributes begin as messy text, and converting them into clean numeric features was essential for all results.

Future work could add marketing data, streaming numbers, social media sentiment, and critic review scores to further improve predictions. Models could also be separated by era (pre- vs post-streaming) or by market (domestic vs international) to capture changing industry dynamics.
